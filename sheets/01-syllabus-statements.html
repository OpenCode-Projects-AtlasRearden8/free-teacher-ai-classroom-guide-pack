Sheet 1

Syllabus Statement Templates

Pick the version that fits your tone and course level, then edit freely. Place the statement in your syllabus near the plagiarism/academic-honesty section, since students read them side by side. Words in brackets are yours to fill in.

Option A — Short version (one small paragraph)

Some assignments in this class allow help from AI tools such as chatbots, and some require you to do the thinking on your own. Each assignment will say clearly which applies. When AI help is allowed, you must describe what you used and how, in a sentence or two attached to your work. Using AI when it is not allowed — or claiming AI work as entirely your own — is treated like any other form of academic dishonesty. We'll talk about what honest AI use looks like in class, and you can always ask me first.

When to use it

Course syllabi with tight page limits, or early in the year when the full policy lives in your assignment handouts.

Option B — Standard version (recommended)

AI tools in this class. AI tools — chatbots, writing assistants, translation apps, image generators, coding assistants, and search features with built-in AI — can be useful. They can also quietly do work we need YOU to do. So this class uses a simple rule: every assignment tells you its AI level on a three-part scale:

  1. AI-Free: no AI help for any part of the work. Your brain, notes, and classmates-as-discussion-partners only.
  2. AI-Assisted: AI may help with brainstorming, revising, checking, or practicing, but the core thinking and writing are yours — and you attach a short AI-use note saying what you used and how.
  3. AI-Included: the assignment is about AI itself (for example, critiquing an AI answer or testing a tool), so using it is part of the work.

If an assignment doesn't state a level, ask before using AI. When AI help is allowed, describe it honestly — including what didn't work. A short, truthful AI-use note never costs you points; a hidden one can. Getting in trouble isn't for using AI; it's for not following the stated rules of the assignment.

When to use it

Middle and high school courses where most of the AI rules live in individual assignments — this pairs directly with Sheet 2 of this pack.

Option C — Expanded version (upper grades)

About AI in [Course Name]. You will graduate into a world full of AI tools, so learning to use them well — and to notice when they fail — is part of what this course teaches. A few commitments from me, so the rules below feel fair:

  • I'll tell you exactly what each assignment expects. No guessing, no trap questions about AI.
  • I'll tell you when I've used AI myself (for example, drafting practice problems or organizing a review sheet), so you see what transparency looks like in practice.
  • Grades go with your thinking, not your fluency with a chatbot. On AI-Assisted work, I'm evaluating the choices you make about what to keep, change, and verify.

What I ask of you:

  1. Follow the level. Every assignment is labeled AI-Free, AI-Assisted, or AI-Included (see the standard version of this policy for definitions).
  2. Check before you trust. AI systems produce confident mistakes. If you quote a fact, name, number, or date from an AI tool, confirm it in a second source — and be ready to point to that source.
  3. Disclose in one honest sentence. Something like: "I used [tool] to brainstorm three opening questions, then wrote and revised the paragraph myself." That's a complete disclosure for most work. What matters is that I could picture your actual process.
  4. Keep your voice. Turning in text a machine can produce doesn't earn credit for the thinking that isn't there. If a paragraph reads exactly like the chatbot's answer and nothing more, it usually is.

Consequences: for a first, minor case (undisclosed but allowed-in-spirit AI help), you'll redo the work with an honest disclosure — no points lost. Repeated or deliberate misrepresentation follows our school's standard academic honesty procedure.

When to use it

Advanced courses, electives, or anywhere students will be using AI tools regularly. The "commitments from me" section tends to buy real goodwill — keep it.

Option D — For families, one sentence you can add anywhere

We use AI tools only where they clearly support learning, always with instructions and transparency; see my syllabus or email me at [your email] with any questions.

Tone check before you commit

Read your final statement and ask: does a student finish reading it knowing exactly what to do on the next assignment? If the answer is "they know AI is involved somehow," add the named levels (Free / Assisted / Included). Naming beats lecturing.

Print-and-sign acknowledgment (optional involvement booster)

Some teachers attach this half-page to the syllabus. Students keep one copy in their binder and return one with a signature. It turns a wall of policy into a two-minute conversation.

AI Use Agreement — [Course Name], [Term]

I've read the AI statement in the syllabus for [Course Name]. I understand that each assignment will be labeled AI-Free, AI-Assisted, or AI-Included, and that when AI help is allowed I'll include a short, honest note about what I used and how.

Short and signable beats long and binding. If students read the levels while signing, the acknowledgment has already done its job.
