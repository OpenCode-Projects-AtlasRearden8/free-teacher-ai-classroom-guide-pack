Assignment Guidelines to Copy

Drop one of the three level cards into any assignment page or handout, then copy the citation format and disclosure form onto the back. Students shouldn't have to wonder which rules apply to tonight's homework — the assignment itself should say.

Level cards to paste into an assignment

AI-FREE assignment. This work is designed to show what you can do right now, on your own. Don't use AI tools — including chatbots, auto-complete writing helpers, translators, or AI search answers — for any part of it. Talking things over with classmates is always fine. If an AI suggestion slips in through your phone's keyboard or browser, that's normal; just mention it in a footnote rather than hiding it.

AI-ASSISTED assignment. You may use AI tools for [brainstorming / outline feedback / grammar checks / practice problems / revised-draft feedback — list what fits this assignment]. You may not use AI to write the final work for you. Attach a short AI-use note (a few sentences is plenty; use the form below for practice). The trust test: describe your process so honestly that I could picture it — including the parts that flopped.

AI-INCLUDED assignment. This assignment is partly about the AI tools themselves. Use them, poke at them, and quote them. What grades your thinking is your analysis of what the tools produced: what was right, wrong, biased, or impressively useful — and how you can tell. Save your actual exchanges with the tool (screenshots or copy-pastes work) so your claims are checkable.

How to label AI use on their work

Print the line below on the bottom of AI-Assisted and AI-Included assignments:

AI-use citation format (adapted for student work):

Tool name. Version or date if known. "What I asked for / gave to the tool." How I used the result.  Examples of good student disclosures:  "I asked a chatbot to quiz me on chapter 4 vocabulary, then retold the causes of the war from memory afterward." · "On my essay draft, I asked AI whether my revisions kept my original main point — I ended up rejecting almost everything it suggested."

Why "how I used it" matters more than the tool name

By the time students graduate, apps will change names constantly. The habit worth teaching is describing one's own process — that transfers to college AI policies, worksites, and publishing platforms alike. Model it once, with your own example of an AI flop, and the disclosures get dramatically more honest.

Grading reminders for you

  • Honest disclosure never lowers a grade. A student confessing "I let it finish my paragraph" with relief is exactly where the lesson lands.
  • Total absence of disclosure on an AI-Free assignment, paired with polished-but-generic output, is a conversation starter — not automatically a referral.
  • Change the level card per assignment, not per student. Nobody wins when the rule is a secret.
