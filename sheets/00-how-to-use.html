Classroom AI Paperwork Pack

How to Use This Pack

A set of ready-to-adapt documents for teachers who are new to AI. Nothing here needs an account, a subscription, or a particular product. Print what you need, change the words to fit your classroom, and keep the pages that work.

What's inside

SheetUse it for
1. Syllabus Statement TemplatesTelling students (and families) up front how AI may and may not be used in your course. Short, medium, and expanded versions.
2. Assignment Guidelines to CopySetting the AI rules for a single assignment: three usage levels, an AI-use citation format, and a short disclosure form students attach.
3. Parent Letter DraftsExplaining your classroom AI approach to families in plain English, with a quick one-paragraph note and a fuller letter.
4. Student Reflection SheetsHelping students think about how they used AI (or chose not to), in their own words. Designed to print double-sided.
5. Access Equity ChecklistA choose-your-own checklist so every student can actually participate, no matter their device, internet, language, or support needs.

Three suggestions before you start

  • Pick one paragraph, not one product. The goal of every sheet is a clear agreement between you and your students. The specific tool names change every year; the standards you set don't.
  • Copy and shrink. Everything is written to be edited. Delete what doesn't apply, and don't feel obligated to use all five sheets in your first year.
  • Model transparency yourself. The most effective syllabus statement pairs with you mentioning, once or twice, when you used AI to make a worksheet or a quiz. Students copy what they see.
Read this first

Check your district or school's own AI policy before distributing anything. If school policy and this pack ever disagree, follow your school's policy and adapt these templates to match it.

Printing notes

  • Everything is sized for US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) and prints fine in black and white.
  • Sheet 4 (student reflection) works doubled-sided: front for the story of the assignment, back for the honest-thinking questions.
  • Checkbox squares and write-on lines are real print elements, so students can fill them in with a pencil.
  • If you prefer to edit on a computer first, open any sheet in a browser, view its source, or copy the text by hand — it's all plain writing on purpose.
